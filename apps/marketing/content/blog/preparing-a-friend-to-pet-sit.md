# How to Prepare a Friend or Neighbour to Look After Your Pet

A professional sitter asks the right questions because it's their job to know what to ask. A friend or neighbour doing you a favour usually won't — not because they don't care, but because they don't know what they don't know yet. That's on you to cover, not them to guess.

## Why this handover is different

With a pro, there's a process. With a mate, it's often a five-minute chat at the door as you're leaving for the airport, and everyone's too polite to ask the follow-up questions that actually matter. That's exactly how the small stuff gets missed — not because anyone was careless, but because nobody wanted to be the one asking "wait, what happens if he won't eat?"

## What to walk through with them

**Do an actual walkthrough, not just a text.** Ten minutes in person beats a wall of text sent from the car. Show them where things are — food, leads, litter, meds — rather than describing it.

**Say the obvious stuff out loud.** Friends assume they'll figure it out. Some of it they will. Feeding amounts, medication timing, and what's actually an emergency versus what's normal are not things to leave to instinct.

**Give them permission, explicitly.** "Call the vet if something's wrong" isn't the same as written authorisation to get treatment and a sense of what you'd spend. Say the actual number you're comfortable with, so they're not guessing under pressure.

**Tell them what's normal.** A friend who's never met your cat before doesn't know that three hours under the bed is standard, not a crisis. Spell out anything that could look worrying but isn't — it saves them a stressful evening and you a middle-of-the-holiday phone call.

**Make it easy to ask.** Tell them explicitly: text you anything, even if it feels dumb. Most people worry about being annoying more than they worry about missing something, so give them permission to over-communicate.

## The bit that's easy to skip with someone you trust

Because it's a friend, not a business transaction, handovers with people you know well tend to be the least documented — which is backwards, since it's often the person least experienced with pet care doing the favour. Trust doesn't replace information. It just means you owe them clear information instead of a vague "you'll be fine."
