# Boarding, a Marketplace Sitter, or a Friend: How to Choose for Your Pet

You've decided you're going away. That was the easy part. Now you're staring at three genuinely different options for your pet — a boarding kennel or cattery, a sitter booked through a marketplace app, or someone you already know — and most of the advice out there just tells you to "pick what's best for your pet" without explaining what actually differs between them.

## The short answer

None of the three is universally better. They differ on where your pet stays, how much routine survives the trip, what it costs, and how much work you have to put in beforehand. The right choice depends on your pet's temperament, how long you're away, and how much handover effort you're willing to do. Here's what actually changes between them.

## Boarding (kennels and catteries)

**What it is:** Your pet stays at a facility, in their own space, looked after by rotating staff.

**Where it's strong:**
- Round-the-clock supervision and on-site help if something goes wrong
- No one in your house, no key handover
- Often the most straightforward option to book last-minute

**Where it's weaker:**
- Your pet is out of their normal environment entirely — new smells, new sounds, other animals nearby
- Routine gets standardised to the facility's schedule, not your pet's
- Higher stress for anxious animals, and for most cats specifically, who tend to do worse with environment change than dogs do
- Staff rotate, so the person feeding your pet on Tuesday may not be the one who fed them Monday

**Best fit:** Confident, sociable dogs who enjoy other animals and don't fuss over routine. Rarely the best fit for anxious cats.

## A marketplace sitter

**What it is:** A vetted, often reviewed, professional sitter you book through an app — either staying in your home or having your pet stay in theirs.

**Where it's strong:**
- Your pet stays in a familiar environment (if the sitter comes to you) or a smaller, calmer one
- Sitters are used to onboarding quickly and know what to ask
- Reviews and vetting give you some confidence in a stranger

**Where it's weaker:**
- It's still a stranger, and the quality of the stay depends heavily on how good your handover is — a sitter can only work with what you tell them
- You're paying for the booking and the trust-building each time, since even a repeat booking usually means the same sitter, but rebuilding rapport with your pet each visit
- Emergency authority and spending limits need to be spelled out explicitly, since a marketplace sitter isn't automatically covered to make decisions on your behalf

**Best fit:** Most pets, most of the time — provided the handover is genuinely complete. This is the option where a good written handover makes the single biggest difference to how the stay goes.

## A friend or neighbour

**What it is:** Someone who already knows you, and sometimes already knows your pet, doing you a favour.

**Where it's strong:**
- Often the most familiar face your pet will see out of the three options
- No cost, and usually more flexibility on timing
- If they've met your pet before, some of the trust is already built

**Where it's weaker:**
- They're doing you a favour, not a job — which means they're less likely to ask the questions a professional would, and more likely to guess
- No vetting, no review history, no built-in process
- Handovers with friends tend to be the least documented of all three, precisely because it feels informal

**Best fit:** Pets who already know the person, and owners willing to do a proper handover rather than a rushed one at the door. The informality is exactly why the information matters more here, not less.

## The variable that matters most, whichever you pick

All three options succeed or fail on the same thing: how complete the handover is. A brilliant marketplace sitter with no information is guessing. A trusted friend with no information is guessing too — just more politely. Boarding facilities are the one place where a written handover doesn't fully substitute for round-the-clock familiarity, but it still shapes how your pet is treated for feeding, medication and anything unusual.

## A quick way to decide

- **Anxious cat, short trip:** friend or marketplace sitter coming to your home, almost always over boarding
- **Confident, social dog, longer trip:** boarding is genuinely fine, sometimes preferable
- **Multiple pets who are used to each other:** a sitter who comes to you keeps their routine and each other intact — splitting them between boarding and a friend adds stress on top of your absence
- **First time leaving this pet with anyone:** whichever option you choose, do the full handover in person, not over text
