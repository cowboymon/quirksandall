# What to Do When a Cat Hides From Their Sitter

Your sitter arrives, and your cat vanishes. No sign of them for hours. If nobody warned the sitter this was likely, this is the moment panic sets in — for the sitter, and then for you, three time zones away with a worried text.

## The short answer

For most cats, disappearing when a stranger arrives is completely normal and not a sign anything's wrong. What matters is what happens after: whether they come out for food, and whether the hiding goes on far longer than usual.

## Why cats hide from new people

Hiding is a cat's default response to anything unfamiliar — new person, new smell, a break in routine. It's not necessarily fear in the way we'd think of it; it's a cat managing an unfamiliar situation on its own terms, in a spot it's chosen because it feels safe there.

## What's normal

- Not being seen for the first few hours of a visit, sometimes longer
- Coming out to eat once the sitter's gone quiet or left the room
- Being seen once, then disappearing again
- Warming up gradually over the first day or two of a multi-day stay

## What's worth mentioning to you or a vet

- No sign of eating, drinking, or using the litter tray for over 24 hours
- Hiding that continues well past your cat's normal settling-in time, with no improvement
- Signs of distress if the sitter can get eyes on them — laboured breathing, obvious pain, not moving normally

The general guidance from vets and cat behaviourists is consistent on this: food and litter tray use are the two things to actually track, because a cat that's eating and toileting normally is very likely just being a cat, even if nobody's laid eyes on them since Tuesday.

## What helps a sitter find out without stressing your cat further

- Knowing your cat's usual hiding spots in advance, so the sitter knows where to check without turning it into a search
- Coming in the same way you usually would (same door, same routine) rather than a route your cat's never associated with you
- Leaving food out and checking it's being eaten, rather than trying to coax a hiding cat into the open
- Not closing doors your cat would normally have access to, so they don't get shut in somewhere unexpected

## What to tell your sitter before you go

Say plainly: "if you don't see her for a day, that's normal — check the food and litter tray, and only worry if neither's being used." That one sentence prevents most of the anxious check-ins you'd otherwise get.
