# What Emergency Information Should Your Pet Sitter Have?

You've covered feeding times and the walk schedule. But if something actually goes wrong — your dog eats something they shouldn't, your cat won't come out and won't eat for a day — does your sitter know who to call, how far they're allowed to go without you, and who decides if you can't be reached?

## The short answer

Five things: your vet's details, an after-hours option, permission to act, a spending limit, and a backup decision-maker. Miss any one of them and an emergency becomes two problems — the actual emergency, and your sitter standing in a vet clinic not sure what they're allowed to do.

## 1. Your regular vet

Name, clinic, address, phone number, and hours. Your sitter needs to be able to get your pet to a vet who already has their history — allergies, past conditions, existing medications — rather than starting from zero with someone new.

## 2. An after-hours or emergency vet

Regular clinics close. If yours does, your sitter needs a second number for after-hours or emergency care, ideally one you've already checked is reasonably close.

## 3. Permission to act

This is the one people skip. "Take them to the vet if needed" isn't permission — it's a suggestion. A sitter who isn't the owner often can't authorise treatment without something in writing, and vets will ask. A short note — *"[Sitter's name] is authorised to seek emergency veterinary treatment for [pet's name] on my behalf while I'm away."* That note isn't a binding legal document — a clinic can still insist on reaching you directly, especially for anything major. But it signals intent clearly and clears the bar for most day-to-day situations.

## 4. A spending limit

Nobody wants their sitter making an on-the-spot decision about a $4,000 procedure with no idea what you'd be comfortable with. Give a number. *"Anything under $500, go ahead. Above that, try me first, then call [backup contact]."* It's not about the money — it's about not putting your sitter in an impossible spot at 11pm.

## 5. A backup decision-maker

If you're unreachable — flight, no signal, time zones — who makes the call? Name one person and make sure your sitter and that person both know they're it.

## What this looks like when it's not written down

It looks like a sitter calling you four times from a vet's waiting room while a receptionist waits for an authorisation you never gave. Most of the time nothing goes wrong on a pet-sitting stint. This is specifically for the times it does — and it's exactly the kind of thing that's easy to forget to mention until it's already 9pm and something's happening.
